One-Stage Reinforcement Learning for Reflection-Equivariant Humanoid Visual Locomotion

In submission to CoRL 2026
Paper arXiv Video Code

Abstract

Humanoid visual locomotion requires learning a policy that turns visual terrain observations, proprioception, and commands into coordinated whole-body actions. Existing methods avoid direct visual policy learning by relying on either blind proprioception or two-stage teacher--student distillation from privileged terrain information. This simplifies training, but mismatched inputs make the visual policy imitate actions not grounded in its own observations.

We propose RePAC, a one-stage RL framework for humanoid visual locomotion. It exploits humanoid reflection symmetry by jointly mirroring terrain observations, proprioception, commands, and motor actions during policy optimization. This situational symmetry provides a dense but soft inductive bias, stabilizing one-stage training without scripted gait timing or rigid equivariant architectures.

Together with ray-traced depth rendering, terrain-centered perception, and multi-scale history, RePAC trains a single whole-body policy across diverse terrains and commands. Empirically, RePAC improves directional tracking, whole-body coordination, terrain traversal, and real-world generalization. These results establish RePAC as a first concrete path to stronger humanoid visual skills through unified perception-action control, beyond distilling from privileged controllers.
